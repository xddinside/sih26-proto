## 2. Evaluation Principle

The project should be evaluated on the basis of:

- demonstrated evidence
- technical merit
- originality
- real-world applicability
- impact
- quality of presentation

**High scores should be supported by observable evidence rather than claims alone.**

---

## 3. Four-Parameter Evaluation Rubric

Each parameter is scored from **1 to 5**.

| No. | Evaluation Parameter | Weight | Evaluation Focus | Score |
|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | **Problem Understanding & Impact** | **25%** | Problem definition, user need, relevance, severity of problem, beneficiary population, societal/industrial impact, and understanding of the prescribed problem statement. | /5 |
| 2 | **Innovation & Technical Excellence** | **30%** | Novelty, USP, differentiation from existing solutions, technical complexity, algorithms/models, architecture, implementation quality, and technology-stack advantage. | /5 |
| 3 | **Feasibility, Practicability & Scalability** | **25%** | Technical and economic feasibility, real-world practicability, deployment readiness, sustainability, scalability, resource requirements, and future productization potential. | /5 |
| 4 | **Solution Quality & Presentation** | **20%** | Prototype quality, UX, clarity, documentation, demonstration, communication, pitch quality, response to questions, and evidence supporting project claims. | /5 |

---

## 4. Score Calculation

Each parameter is scored from **1 to 5**.

### Formula

\[
\text{Final Score} = \sum_{i=1}^{4}\left(\frac{\text{Parameter Score}_i}{5} \times \text{Weight}_i\right)
\]

Weights are treated as marks out of 100:

| Parameter | Weight | Score / 5 | Weighted Marks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Problem Understanding & Impact | 25 | | |
| Innovation & Technical Excellence | 30 | | |
| Feasibility, Practicability & Scalability | 25 | | |
| Solution Quality & Presentation | 20 | | |
| **TOTAL** | **100** | | **/100** |

---

## 5. Five-Level Evaluation Scale

| Score | Rating | Evaluator Guidance |
|---:|---|---|
| **5** | **Outstanding** | Exceptional evidence, highly convincing, innovative and technically strong. |
| **4** | **Very Good** | Strong performance with minor gaps that do not materially affect the solution. |
| **3** | **Good** | Adequate and credible solution with identifiable areas for improvement. |
| **2** | **Below Average** | Significant weaknesses, limited evidence or incomplete implementation. |
| **1** | **Poor** | Major deficiencies, weak evidence or insufficient demonstration. |

---

## 6. Final Score Interpretation

| Final Score | Rating | Interpretation |
|---:|---|---|
| **90-100** | **Outstanding** | Strong candidate for SIH selection; highly competitive project. |
| **81-89** | **Very Good** | Strong candidate with limited areas requiring refinement. |
| **71-80** | **Good** | Promising project requiring targeted improvements. |
| **51-70** | **Needs Improvement** | Significant improvements required before selection. |
| **0-50** | **Weak** | Major deficiencies in problem, technology, feasibility or execution. |

## 10. USP and Innovation Check

The evaluator should specifically verify:

- Is the proposed solution genuinely differentiated?
- Is the Unique Selling Proposition clearly stated?
- Does the solution improve upon existing approaches?
- Is there meaningful technical or domain-specific innovation?
- Does the technology stack directly address the identified bottleneck?
- Is the project more than a generic CRUD application or basic AI wrapper?
